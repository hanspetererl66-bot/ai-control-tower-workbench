# Post 4 – Job posting and recruiting (voice: Dana, ~45, American English; music: light 1960s jazz)
Intro and outro as in the pre-trailer.

## Job posting and recruiting, start to finish

Hey y'all, I'm Dana, and I'm a Design Engineer! Design Study twenty twenty-six: the whole hiring process in one Space, from the job posting to the offer.

These are possible design proposals within an MVP. Sample data, not a finished product.

## One page for the whole process

So, here's the deal. Every step lives on one page: write the posting, publish it, sort the candidates, interview, and get the offer approved.

The dashboard shows where things stand: open jobs, applications by channel, and how long it all takes.

## Start from the requisition

You start with the requisition. Job title, department, location, the must-haves and the nice-to-haves. Joule never fills in the pay range. That one is on you.

## Draft with Joule

Next, pick a template. Standard, shift roles, IT consulting, or one of your own. Hit Draft with Joule, and it writes every section from your brief.

And here's the cool part: it invents no tasks, no benefits, and no pay. Whatever it doesn't know stays an empty field.

## Edit and check

Then you edit. The company text is locked, so nobody can mess with the legal parts. And the check tells you straight: one thing to fix, the empty pay range. No pay, no release.

## Release and publish

Once it's clean, the hiring manager releases it. Then one click publishes to five channels: the career portal, LinkedIn, Xing, Monster and company social posts. Each text fits its channel's limit.

Heads up, nothing is really sent. The channels are simulated in this study.

## The pipeline

Applications roll in, and the pipeline shows each stage, from new to offered, with the days each person has waited.

Open a candidate and you see the profile, but not the name. Names and contact details are removed on purpose, so the decision is about skills.

## Match agent

The match agent compares the CV with the criteria, and tells you which musts are met. It ranks, but it never rejects anybody. A person makes that call, every single time.

## Online interview

Now the online interview. The hiring manager approves the questions first. Then the candidate talks to an AI avatar, and gets told up front that it is an AI.

The avatar is a mock here, a real one comes later. And a person can always say, I would rather talk to a person.

## Offer approvals

Then the offer. Joule proposes, the chain approves: hiring manager, H R, finance, department head. You see who signed, who is next, and you can approve, return it, or decline.

## Time to fill, brief, guardrails

Time to fill shows which requisitions are late. The morning brief says what needs you today. Ask Joule answers in plain words.

And the guardrails list, on one page, what Joule does and does not do. It does not decide, and it does not send anything out.

## Closing

Posting, publishing, pipeline, interview and offer. One process, and a person decides at every step.
